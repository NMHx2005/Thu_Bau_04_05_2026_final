Still Warm

The coat was still on the hook by the door. I knew the weave before I touched it, the way the collar held its shape after someone shrugged it on in a hurry.

It smelled like rain and something sweeter underneath, like skin and detergent and the last hour of a day that did not end cleanly. My hand closed on the sleeve and the fabric was warm, as if a body had just left it.

I stood there longer than I meant to, carrying the weight in both hands, remembering a night on a step outside a library when you laughed and did not pull away. The coat should have been cold by now. It was not.
